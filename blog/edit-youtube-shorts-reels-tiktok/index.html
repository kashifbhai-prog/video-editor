How to edit vertical videos for YouTube Shorts, Reels and TikTok

{{postMeta}}

Short vertical videos are watched on a phone, often with the sound off, by someone whose thumb is already halfway to the next video. That changes how you should edit. This guide walks through a complete workflow – from setting up the canvas to exporting – and explains the reasons behind each step, so you can adapt it to your own content.

1. Start with a 9:16 canvas

Shorts, Reels and TikTok all use the same shape: a tall 9:16 frame, which is 1080 × 1920 pixels in Full HD. Set this before you start cutting, because the position and size of every title, sticker and caption depends on it.

In Cutistic, click an empty part of the timeline so nothing is selected. The Project tab on the right shows the canvas settings – choose 9:16 as the aspect ratio. If you also publish a horizontal version for YouTube, finish the vertical edit first and make a copy later; it is much easier to widen a vertical edit than to squeeze a horizontal one.

Tip

Frame rate: keep the rate your camera recorded at – usually 30 fps on phones. Changing 30 fps footage to 24 fps makes motion look slightly jerky and gains you nothing on social platforms.

2. Reframe horizontal footage properly

If your clip was filmed horizontally, it will appear as a thin strip in the middle of the tall frame with large black bars above and below. You have three options:

  • Fill the frame. Select the clip and click Fill canvas. The picture is enlarged until it covers the whole frame, and the left and right sides are cropped. Then drag the picture in the player (or change Position X) so the person or object you care about is in the centre. This is the best choice for a single person talking.
  • Fit with a background. Keep Fit canvas so the whole picture is visible, and put something behind it: a colour, a blurred copy of the same clip on a lower track (scaled up with a blur effect), or a title above and below. This works for wide shots you don't want to crop, such as a screen recording.
  • Follow the action. When the subject moves across the frame, use keyframes on Position X: add a keyframe (the small ◇ next to the setting) where the movement starts, move the playhead, drag the picture so the subject is centred again. Cutistic moves the frame smoothly between the two positions.

Whatever you choose, make sure faces are not cut off at the top. People look at eyes first; leave a little space above the head rather than a lot.

3. The first three seconds decide everything

On short-video feeds, viewers decide very quickly whether to keep watching. The most common mistake is a slow opening: a logo, a "hi guys", a wide shot of a room. Instead:

  • Start with the most interesting moment – the result, the surprise, the question. You can always explain how you got there afterwards.
  • Put a short title on screen in the first frame. Many people scroll with the sound off, so a few words such as "3 mistakes in your CV" tell them why to stay. Use the Text panel and a bold style; keep it to one line if you can.
  • Cut the breath before the first word. Zoom the timeline in (press =), find the exact frame where speech starts and trim the clip there with Q.

4. Add captions – most people watch without sound

Captions are not optional on vertical video. They keep muted viewers watching, help people who are hard of hearing or watching in a second language, and make fast speech easier to follow.

Typing them by hand takes a long time, so let the computer write the first draft. Open the Captions panel, choose the language that is spoken and click Generate captions. The speech model runs on your own computer and the captions appear on a caption track, timed to the voice. Then:

  1. Read every caption once. Names, brands and technical words are the usual mistakes. Click a caption's time to jump to it and correct the text in the list.
  2. Make them readable on a phone. In Style, choose a large, bold font, a strong outline or a semi-transparent background box, and white or yellow text.
  3. Place them in the lower-middle of the frame. The bottom part of the screen is covered by the platform's own buttons, the account name and the description. Captions placed a little below the centre are safe on every app.

For more detail – including Urdu and other languages – see our guide How to add Urdu subtitles to a video and the help article on auto captions.

5. Respect the safe zones

Each app draws its interface on top of your video: like and comment buttons down the right side, the caption and music name across the bottom, sometimes a search bar at the top. Anything important you place there may be hidden. A simple rule that works for Shorts, Reels and TikTok alike:

Area of the 9:16 frameWhat to put there
Top ~10%Nothing essential. Background only.
MiddleFaces, products, titles and captions.
Right edgeKeep text away from it – the action buttons sit here.
Bottom ~20%Nothing essential. The account name and description cover it.

Before exporting, play the video in the player and imagine those buttons on top. If a word or a face would be covered, move it.

6. Keep the pace up

Vertical video rewards a faster rhythm than a normal YouTube video. That does not mean frantic editing; it means nothing on screen without a reason.

  • Remove the pauses. In AI tools › Silence removal, click Detect silence. Every pause appears as a red band on the timeline. Untick the ones you want to keep (a dramatic pause, a laugh) and remove the rest in one click. Picture, music and captions stay in sync.
  • Change the picture every few seconds. A cut to a close-up, a screenshot, a product shot or even a slight zoom on the same clip resets the viewer's attention. To "punch in", split the clip (S) and set the second part's Scale to around 115%.
  • Cut repeated takes. If you recorded a line several times, AI tools › Smart cuts can spot scenes that look alike and suggest removing the earlier take.

Our article on editing talking-head videos covers these techniques in more depth.

7. Music and sound

Music gives a short video energy, but it must never compete with the voice. Put the music on its own audio track and lower it to around 10–20% while someone speaks. If the music should rise at the end, add volume keyframes: one where the speech ends at the low level, and one a second later at a higher level.

If your voice was recorded on a phone in a noisy room, select the clip and try AI tools › Audio enhancement: AI noise removal removes fan and traffic noise, and Normalise speech loudness makes the voice loud enough for phone speakers. Compare Original and Enhanced before you apply it. There are more tips in 9 ways to make your voice sound better.

Copyright

Only use music you have the right to use. Songs you bought or streamed are usually not licensed for publishing, and platforms may mute your video or block it. Use music from a free library that allows it, or the platform's own sound library when you post.

8. Length and ending

There is no perfect length. A video should be exactly as long as its idea – a single tip may need 15 seconds, a recipe 60. Watch your edit once from the start, without stopping, and cut anything where your own attention drops.

Endings matter too. Short videos often play in a loop, so a clean ending that flows back into the opening makes people watch twice. Avoid long "subscribe" outros; a single line of text such as "Part 2 tomorrow" is enough.

9. Export settings for vertical video

Click Export (Ctrl + E). For Shorts, Reels and TikTok these settings are a safe choice:

SettingRecommendedWhy
Resolution1080p (1080 × 1920)The size all three apps display. 4K is converted down anyway.
Frame rateSame as your footage (usually 30)Avoids jerky motion.
CodecH.264Accepted everywhere.
PresetStandard or High qualityEnough bitrate that the app's own compression doesn't make it blocky.

The apps re-compress everything you upload, so a slightly higher bitrate than you think you need gives them a cleaner starting point. Our guide to the best export settings explains each option in detail.

A quick checklist before you post

  • Canvas is 9:16 and nothing important is cut off.
  • The first second shows the most interesting thing, with a title on screen.
  • Captions are corrected, readable and above the bottom 20%.
  • Pauses and repeated takes are gone.
  • Music is quiet under speech and you have the right to use it.
  • Exported at 1080 × 1920, H.264, at the original frame rate.
