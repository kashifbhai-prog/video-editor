9 ways to make your voice sound better in videos (without buying a new mic)

{{postMeta}}

Viewers forgive a slightly soft picture. They do not forgive bad sound. A voice that is echoey, buzzing or too quiet makes people click away within seconds – even when the content is great. The good news: most audio problems are cheap to fix. The first five tips cost nothing and happen before you press record; the last four happen in the edit.

Part 1: Record it right

No software can fully rebuild a voice that was badly recorded. Ten minutes of preparation saves an hour of repair – and produces a more natural result than any filter.

1. Choose a soft room, not a big one

The biggest difference between "amateur" and "professional" voice audio is usually echo, not the microphone. Hard, empty rooms – tiled kitchens, bare offices, halls – bounce your voice off every wall and the microphone records all of it.

Record in the softest room you have: a bedroom with a bed, curtains, a carpet and a full wardrobe is far better than a bigger, emptier room. If you must record in a bare room, put cushions, blankets or a sofa near you, and face into the room's softest corner. Even hanging a thick blanket behind the camera helps.

2. Turn off what hums

Fans, air conditioners, refrigerators and computers make a steady background noise that you stop noticing after a minute – but your recording does not. Switch off what you can for the length of a take. Close windows facing a road. Put your phone in airplane mode so notifications don't interrupt you.

3. Get the microphone close

Distance matters more than price. A phone's built-in microphone 30 cm from your mouth often sounds better than an expensive camera microphone two metres away, because the closer the microphone is, the louder your voice is compared with the room.

  • Phone on a tripod in front of you? Record the audio separately on a second phone (in a voice-memo app) held or placed just out of shot, about a hand-span from your mouth.
  • Clip-on (lavalier) microphone? Clip it to your shirt about a hand-span below your chin, away from jewellery and fabric that can rub.
  • Desk microphone? Speak slightly past it rather than straight into it, to avoid popping "p" and "b" sounds.

If you record audio on a separate device, clap once on camera at the start. In the editor, line up the spike of the clap in the audio waveform with the frame where your hands meet, then mute the camera's own sound.

4. Set the level: loud, but never too loud

A recording that is too quiet sounds noisy when you boost it later. A recording that is too loud clips – the peaks are cut off and the voice crackles, and that cannot be repaired. Aim for your loudest moments to stay comfortably below the maximum. Many apps show a level meter; if yours does, keep normal speech in the upper-middle of the meter and make sure laughter or a raised voice never hits the end.

When you record a voice-over in Cutistic (Audio › Record voice-over), watch the level meter while you speak, and do a short test take first.

5. Record ten seconds of silence

Before you start talking, stay quiet for ten seconds. This "room tone" is useful later: noise-removal tools can learn what your room sounds like, and you can use a piece of it to fill a gap where you cut something out, so the background doesn't suddenly go dead.

Part 2: Fix it in the edit

Once the recording is as good as you can make it, editing can make it sound clean and polished. The order matters: clean first, then shape, then set the loudness.

6. Remove background noise

For steady noise – fans, traffic, hiss, computer hum – AI noise removal does an excellent job. In Cutistic, select the clip with your voice and open AI tools › Audio enhancement, then switch on AI noise removal. It uses a neural network trained to separate speech from noise, so it works even underneath your words, not only in the pauses.

Click Original and Enhanced to compare eight seconds before you apply anything. If the voice starts to sound thin or "underwater", lower the Strength – a little background left in usually sounds more natural than total silence.

Noise removal cannot remove echo from a bare room or someone talking in the background – that is why tips 1 and 2 matter.

7. Shape the voice with EQ

An equaliser (EQ) makes some frequencies louder or quieter. You do not need to be an audio engineer; three small moves fix most voices:

ProblemWhat to do
Rumble from traffic, desk bumps or windLower the 60 Hz band a lot. A speaking voice has almost nothing that low.
Boomy, "boxy" voice from a small roomLower 250 Hz a little (a few dB).
Muffled, hard to understandRaise 4 kHz a little – this is where the clarity of consonants lives.
Harsh "s" soundsLower 12 kHz slightly.

You find these bands under the clip's Audio section. If you prefer a one-click option, Voice clarity in Audio enhancement offers Light, Clear voice and Strong (podcast) presets that remove rumble and boominess and add presence for you. Make small changes and listen on headphones and on a phone speaker – that is where most of your audience will hear it.

8. Make the loudness consistent

Viewers should never have to touch the volume button. Two problems are common: the whole video is too quiet compared with others on the platform, and different clips – or different speakers – have different levels.

  • Normalise speech loudness (in Audio enhancement) brings a voice to a consistent, loud-enough level and includes a limiter, so peaks never distort.
  • Balance all clip volumes measures the speech in every clip and sets their gain so all speakers sound equally loud – ideal for interviews and videos made from several recordings.

Large platforms adjust the playback volume of loud videos down to a common level, so making your video louder than everyone else's does not help – but a video that is too quiet stays too quiet. Consistent, healthy speech loudness is the goal.

9. Keep music under the voice – and cut the dead air

Background music should support the voice, never compete with it. As a starting point, set music to around 10–20% volume under speech and listen; if you have to concentrate to understand the words, it is still too loud. Fade the music in and out (Fade in / Fade out in the clip's audio settings) rather than starting it abruptly. For music that rises between sentences, add volume keyframes with the ◇ button.

Finally, long pauses make even good audio feel slow. AI tools › Silence removal finds every pause, shows them on the timeline and removes the ones you choose. Set Keep around speech so a little breathing room remains – cutting too tightly makes speech sound unnatural.

A five-minute audio routine

  1. Record in a soft, quiet room with the microphone close, plus ten seconds of silence.
  2. In the edit, apply AI noise removal to the voice and compare before and after.
  3. Apply a Voice clarity preset, or lower 60 Hz and raise 4 kHz slightly.
  4. Normalise speech loudness, or balance all clip volumes for several speakers.
  5. Put music at 10–20% under speech with fades, and remove long pauses.
  6. Listen once on headphones and once on a phone speaker before exporting.

All of these tools are free in Cutistic and run on your computer, so your recordings are never uploaded. For every audio setting, see the help article on audio editing.
